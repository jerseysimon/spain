The hut-to-hut

Val d'Aran horseshoe

Restanca → Colomers, van based at Vielha · Aigüestortes i Estany de Sant Maurici · three days, two nights · Sat 22 – Mon 24 August 2026.

The shape of it: three days of walking, two nights in lakeside mountain huts, entirely on the Val d'Aran side of the park. We base the van at the Val d'Aran Camper Park (Vielha) and never move it — a short taxi takes us to the start on Day 1, and a reliable 4×4 jeep shuttle brings us out on Day 3. No long exit day, no crossing the whole park, no doubling back.

Route at a glance

Pont de Ressec → Refugi dera Restanca → (Port de Caldes) → Refugi de Colomers → Banhs de Tredòs. A west-to-east horseshoe: up one Aran valley, over the scenic Port de Caldes, down the neighbouring Aran valley.

Why it's doable

The simplest way to picture it: nothing on this route is harder than what we already did in Austria last August. On 25 August 2025 we climbed almost 1,000 m in a day with the kids and took it in our stride — six hours door to door with plenty of stops. The biggest climb here is well under half of that, and the last day is almost all downhill.

Ascent is what drives effort. Our Leutasch big day was +972 m; the hardest day here is +615 m, and it's the first day — after that it only gets easier. The shape of each day matches Austria: walk a few hours, stop often, reach the hut by mid-afternoon, spend the rest of the day by a lake. The one thing to know: Day 2 is graded F ("easy" on the alpine scale) — a stony, sure-footed high stage with brief exposed moments near the pass, but nothing to scramble or hold onto.

The Day 3 pickup is the one to lock in. Banhs de Tredòs is the top of a dead-end road with no rank, so a driver must be sent up. Booking ahead with a named driver (and Radio Taxi as backup) removes the only real uncertainty in the plan.

  • Day 0: arrive at Val d'Aran Camper Park, Vielha. Park, hook up, sleep.
  • Day 1 morning: taxi from Vielha up the Valarties to Pont de Ressec / Pontet de Rius (~25–30 min) and start walking.
  • Day 3 midday: jeep down to Banhs de Tredòs, then taxi back to Vielha (~35–40 min via Salardú). The van's been sitting at the Camper Park the whole time.

Rough cost sense: two Aran-valley taxi hops (Vielha → Pont de Ressec, Banhs de Tredòs → Vielha) plus the ~€5 pp jeep shuttle. The Aran taxi network covers both routes as standard — confirm current prices when booking.

Booking the huts — do this first

Both huts are booked through refusonline.com. Reserve Restanca (night 1) and Colomers (night 2) for the nights of 22 and 23 August, in one session, then phone to confirm.

  • Phone: +34 973 641 681 (Mon–Fri, 9:00–17:00 in season).
  • Put our daughter on the booking so meals are catered, and ask for a family room.
  • Book early — both sit on the Carros de Foc circuit and fill fast in August; the huts' own advice is at least a month ahead.

What to carry

  • Sleeping-bag liner each (mandatory — huts provide mattresses and blankets, not bedding).
  • Cash — most huts want cash for extras, and the jeep prefers it too.
  • Light layers, waterproof, sun protection, refillable water, head-torch, small first-aid, blister kit.
  • Poles help on Day 2's stony descent and the Day 1 forest climb.
  • Proper mountain rescue cover — a helicopter evacuation for a sprained ankle is expensive.
  • Day packs only — two nights of hut meals means light bags, no camping kit.

Good to know

  • No swimming in the lakes or rivers inside the national park — worth telling the children before they see the water.
  • No signal at the huts. Download the tracks (Wikiloc: Pont de Ressec–Restanca, and Restanca–Colomers GR11.18) and cache offline maps before we go.
  • Confirm the Banhs de Tredòs jeep is running on our dates — it's a summer service, and the Day 3 exit leans on it.
  • No camping / bivouac in the park, so there's no fallback if a hut booking falls through — another reason to confirm by phone.
  • Nights are cool at ~2,000–2,100 m even in August — pack a warm layer for the huts.
  • Route: GR11 approach (Pont de Ressec–Restanca) + GR11.18 stage 24 (Restanca–Colomers, via Port de Caldes) + Colomers descent to Banhs de Tredòs. Tracks on Wikiloc and travesiapirenaica.com. This is a planning summary — navigate from the GPX and a proper map on the day.

🇫🇷 France — the club model

FR1 · Belambra "Les Ayguades"

Gruissan · ★3.3 (628) · club, half/full board

An active family club on a big sandy beach with good kids'/teens' clubs (3–17) — and it slots neatly into the route, ~1 hr from Collioure, ~45 min from Carcassonne. Reviewers find the site a touch dated, which drags the score.

Heads-up: kids' clubs run during French school holidays only — our late-August dates should fall inside them, but confirm the end date.

+33 4 68 75 17 17 · belambra.fr

FR2 · Belambra "Les Salins"

Port Camargue · ★4.0 (1,030) · club, premium

The best-rated French pick — brand new (2022) with three pools and shallow, kid-friendly beach water. Pairs well with a Rhône-valley route north (Pont du Gard as a bonus) rather than the Carcassonne/Millau line.

Heads-up: pricier tier and a detour east of our drawn route; a few find rooms small. Bring mosquito repellent.

+33 4 66 80 00 10 · belambra.fr

FR3 · Club Med Opio en Provence

Opio, Provence · ★4.1 (2,620) · true all-inclusive

The genuine all-inclusive — meals, drinks, shows, flying trapeze and kids'/teens' clubs from 4 months. A stunning setting with praised food and activities the kids love.

Heads-up: well off the northward route — only sensible if we finish on the Côte d'Azur. Brand premium.

+33 4 93 09 71 00 · clubmed.com

🇪🇸 Spain — the Costa Brava

ES1 · Cala Montjoi, Roses

Roses · ★4.4 (3,784) · holiday village, buffet

A holiday village tucked in a cove inside the Cap de Creus natural park — buffet dining, kids' clubs, its own diving school and hiking straight from the door. ~1 hr south of Collioure, the closest and prettiest, with the legendary elBulli site hidden in the same bay. Families return year after year.

Heads-up: a rocky cove rather than a big sandy beach, and the water runs cooler this far north — more nature-retreat than resort.

+34 972 25 62 12 · montjoi.com

ES2 · Hotel Spa Mediterráneo Park, Roses

Roses · ★4.5 (2,302) · hotel, semi-AI

A proper hotel close to the border — three pools, a shin-deep kids' pool with a slide, a kids' club running late, and a good buffet. Spotless, spacious and well-staffed.

Heads-up: the "all-inclusive" really only covers drinks at breakfast — check exactly what the package includes. Crowd skews older and quieter.

Things to do

Ideas along the route

What each area we're passing through is good for — plenty to fill the three open nights, and more than enough for the booked ones. Grouped by leg, west to east.

Basque Country · Legs 1–2 (Bilbao & San Sebastián)

townBilbao — the Guggenheim and its "Puppy", the Casco Viejo old town and the Ribera food market; ride the Funicular de Artxanda for the city view.
eatPintxos crawl — bar-hop the Parte Vieja in San Sebastián, then whole turbot grilled over coals at Getaria down the coast.
beachLa Concha & Zurriola — the golden city bay and the surf beach; the old clifftop fairground up Monte Igueldo by funicular.
day tripSan Juan de Gaztelugatxe — the dramatic islet chapel (Dragonstone in Game of Thrones), a 241-step causeway near Bermeo, west of Bilbao.
walkFlysch coast, Zumaia — striped rock strata and a UNESCO geopark, right on the way east toward Ondres.

French Basque & Landes coast · Leg 3 (Ondres)

beachAtlantic surf — long beaches at Ondres, Labenne and Hossegor; the Vélodyssée cycle path threads through the Landes pine forest.
townBayonne & Biarritz — Bayonne's old town, Gothic cathedral and Basque chocolate; Biarritz for the Grande Plage and the Rocher de la Vierge.
kidsAquarium de Biarritz — sharks, seals and a rooftop terrace; or a first surf lesson on the Côte des Landes.
day tripBack to San Sebastián — only ~40 min south over the border if we want another round of pintxos.

Catalan Pyrenees · Leg 4 (Val d'Aran & Aigüestortes)

walkSant Maurici — beyond the booked hut-to-hut, easy day-loops around the lake under the Encantats; jeep-taxi in from Espot or Boí.
townVielha & the Aran valley — Romanesque churches and the Uelhs deth Joèu waterfalls at Artiga de Lin.
cultureVall de Boí — UNESCO Romanesque churches (Sant Climent de Taüll) with the thermal baths at Caldes de Boí next door.
waterRafting the Noguera Pallaresa — Spain's classic whitewater from Sort, about an hour south.
wildlifeMarmots & izard — whistling marmots and Pyrenean chamois are common in the national park.

Languedoc coast · Leg 5 (Sérignan)

beachSérignan-Plage & Valras — the booked beach and family-friendly Valras next door; flamingos in the coastal lagoons.
cultureBéziers — the nine locks of Fonseranes on the Canal du Midi (UNESCO) — cycle or cruise the towpath.
townPézenas & Narbonne — Molière's craft town, and Roman Narbonne with its covered market, Les Halles.
eatÉtang de Thau oysters — straight from the water at Bouzigues or Mèze, out toward Sète.
day tripOppidum d'Ensérune — a hilltop pre-Roman town with sweeping views, a short hop inland.

The run north · Leg 6 (toward Calais)

castleCarcassonne — La Cité's fairytale ramparts, ~1½ hr from the coast — a natural first stop out of Sérignan.
bridgeMillau viaduct — the viewpoint below then drive across the top; swim and canoe the Tarn gorges to break the drive.
buildGuédelon — a 13th-century castle being built from scratch by hand with period tools, in Burgundy — brilliant for kids.
wineBeaune or the Loire — Burgundy wine town, or a château (Chambord, Chenonceau) if the route swings west.
finalReims & Champagne — the cathedral and the cellars, on the last approach to Calais.

Friends who know these coasts

Alex and Anna have done the northern coast and the Costa Brava. First in is Alexandra's full San Sebastián food list — the insider version of Leg 2's pintxos night — with more north-coast and Costa Brava tips to drop in as they come.

San Sebastián — Alexandra's food list

Pintxos

Order the dish, not just the bar — mushrooms at Gambara, braised cheek at San Telmo, Idiazábal risotto at Borda Berri, anchovy with spider crab at Txepetxa, and octopus at Atari (a lovely terrace onto the old church square — book well ahead).

Lunch trips

Turbot at Kaia Kaipe in Getaria, or txuleta steak at Casa Nicolás in Tolosa — both scenic drives, both need booking (~€80–100pp). Casual pick: Bedua for chipirones and home-grown veg (~€40–60pp).

Breakfast

Bar Antonio on Calle Bergara (not the Boulevard branch) — the town's best potato tortilla; go 11am–noon before it sells out.

Something sweet

Burnt Basque cheesecake at La Viña (any time), and pistachio ice cream at Paperino in Gros.

Drinks

Terraces on Plaza de la Constitución pre- or post-dinner, and the wine-and-tapas buzz along Calle Peña y Goñi in Gros.

Between meals

Chillida's Peine del Viento sculpture, the Kursaal and Miramar Palace, and Monte Igueldo for the views over the bay.

Santander

Go here for the seafood — the standout on the north coast, Santander eastward.
